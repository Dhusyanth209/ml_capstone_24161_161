Model Interpretation & amp; Experimental Graphs

The core results of the Grover Policy Agent (GPA) paper (arxiv:2502.13755) center around four key quantitative benchmarks proving quantum advantage in sensor circuit synthesis.

Fig. 6: QFI vs Grover Rotations (Base 2-Qubit QSC)

Model Interpretation:

In the 2-qubit Base QSC, the agent searches over squeezing angle pairs (theta_1, theta_2). As Grover amplitude amplification iterates, the probability amplitude of states yielding maximal Quantum Fisher Information (maximum entangled N00N-like states) approaches 1.0. At the optimal theoretical bound of k = 50 Grover rotations, the QFI peaks exactly at 1.0, matching the Heisenberg-limited sensitivity.

Fig. 8: QFI vs Grover Rotations (Simplified 3-Qubit QSC)

Model Interpretation:

To reduce circuit complexity for NISQ devices, multi-qubit squeezing is replaced with Controlled-V gates and single-qubit rotations. Because of ancilla dilution and approximation bounds, the maximal QFI attainable is 0.5 at k = 40 rotations. This confirms that GPA converges to the theoretically pareto-optimal circuit under gate-decomposition constraints.

Fig. 7: QPE Mean Absolute Error (MAE) vs Counting Qubits

Model Interpretation:

Quantum Phase Estimation (QPE) is used to evaluate the value function V_pi(s). The Mean Absolute Error (MAE) exhibits the predicted exponential decrease O$2^-t$ as the counting register size increases, reaching < 0.004 at 8 counting qubits.

Fig. 10: GPA vs GAQA Baseline Comparison

Model Interpretation:

This comparison demonstrates the primary novelty of GPA. Under an identical rotation budget of 40 rotations, GPA achieves QFI = 0.50 compared to the GAQA baseline which only reaches QFI = 0.45 (an 11.1% advantage). This occurs because GAQA sequentially allocates budgets to individual gates, leading to error compounding, whereas GPA searches over the joint policy space using Grover amplification.
